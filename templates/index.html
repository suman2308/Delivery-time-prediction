The problem

Why courier delivery times are so hard to predict

Every shipment is a bet between the promise and reality — until the data gets a say.

Delays happen everywhere

Weather, traffic, hub congestion, weekend cut-offs and parcel size all shift real delivery times. Static promises can't keep up.

Guesswork is expensive

Under-promising loses customers; over-promising breaks trust and inflates escalations. Either way, margin leaks out of every lane.

AI changes the game

A model trained on real shipment history learns how each route, mode and parcel profile actually behaves — and predicts within hours.

How it works

From shipment details to explainable ETA

Five steps. Under a second of compute. Zero guesswork.

1 · Enter shipment details

Origin, destination, mode, parcel weights and booking day.

2 · AI processing

The model encodes the route and parcels, then scores the shipment against 49,639 historical records.

3 · Prediction

A delivery-duration estimate in days, with model version attached.

4 · Explanation

Confidence, risk level and the inputs that drove the estimate.

5 · Download report

Export the result for your records or customer communication.

FAQ

Questions, answered

The model achieves a mean absolute error of 0.54 days (≈13 hours) on a held-out test set, with an R² of 0.75. Accuracy varies by route and mode — air and express lanes tend to be tighter than surface.

Every account starts on the free plan with 50 predictions per month — the counter resets on the 1st. Once you reach 50, upgrade to Pro (₹299/month) or Pro+ (₹1,299/year) for unlimited predictions, applied instantly with demo billing.

The model was trained on 30+ Indian cities including Mumbai, Delhi, Bangalore, Kolkata, Chennai, Hyderabad, Pune, and Ahmedabad. Unknown cities are handled gracefully by the encoder rather than crashing.

Register for an account and you'll get a scp_live_ API key instantly. Send it as X-API-Key on POST /api/predict with the same fields as the web form, and get JSON back with the predicted days, model version, and algorithm.

Every prediction is written to an audit table with its full input vector, predicted duration, timestamp, and model version — so you always know exactly what drove each estimate and when it was made.
